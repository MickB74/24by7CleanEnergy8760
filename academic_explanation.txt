# Theoretical Framework and Algorithmic Specification of the 8760 Clean Energy Simulator

## Abstract
This document delineates the mathematical architecture and computational methodologies underpinning the "8760 Clean Energy Simulator." The system is a stochastic discrete-time simulation engine designed to model, optimize, and value "24/7 Carbon-Free Energy" (CFE) procurement portfolios. Operating at an hourly temporal resolution ($t \in [0, 8760]$), the simulator integrates probabilistic load modeling, parametric renewable generation synthesis, heuristic storage dispatch, and scarcity-based financial valuation to assess the efficacy of hybrid energy portfolios (Solar, Wind, Nuclear, Geothermal, Hydro, BESS) in achieving decarbonization targets.

## 1. Stochastic Load Modeling Methodology
The system constructs aggregate demand profiles through a bottom-up summation of distinct building typologies, each characterized by a deterministic diurnal base load, a seasonal thermal conditioning component, and stochastic variability.

### 1.1 Base Load Construction
For each building typology $k$ (e.g., Data Center, Office, Residential), a normalized diurnal profile $P_{base}^k(h)$ is defined for $h \in [0, 23]$. The hourly load $L^k(t)$ at simulation hour $t$ is derived as:

$$ L^k(t) = P_{base}^k(t \pmod{24}) \times S^k(d_t) \times (1 + \epsilon_t) $$

Where:
- $d_t$ is the day of the year corresponding to hour $t$.
- $S^k(d_t)$ is the seasonal scaling factor.
- $\epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ represents Gaussian white noise with $\sigma=0.02$ (2% variability).

### 1.2 Seasonality Functions
Thermal conditioning loads are modeled via sinusoidal functions calibrated to building thermodynamics.
- **Data Centers**: Exhibit minimal seasonality due to constant internal cooling loads.
  $$ S^{DC}(d) = 1.0 + 0.05 \cos\left(\frac{2\pi(d - 200)}{365}\right) $$
- **Residential**: Exhibits high seasonality driven by HVAC requirements.
  $$ S^{Res}(d) = 1.0 + 0.40 \cos\left(\frac{2\pi(d - 200)}{365}\right) $$
- **General Commercial**:
  $$ S^{Com}(d) = 1.0 + 0.20 \cos\left(\frac{2\pi(d - 200)}{365}\right) $$

The phase shift $\phi=200$ aligns peak demand with mid-July (approx. Day 200).

## 2. Parametric Renewable Generation Synthesis
Renewable generation profiles are synthesized using stochastic parametric models calibrated to regional meteorological characteristics (e.g., ERCOT, CAISO).

### 2.1 Solar Photovoltaic (PV) Model
Solar output $G_{solar}(t)$ is modeled as a function of solar geometry, seasonal declination, and stochastic cloud attenuation.

$$ G_{solar}(t) = C_{solar} \times I_{geo}(t) \times S_{solar}(d_t) \times (1 - \beta_t \cdot \kappa_{cloud}) $$

Where:
- $I_{geo}(t) = \max(0, \sin(\frac{\pi(h_t - 6)}{12}))$ for $h_t \in [6, 18]$, representing the diurnal insolation curve.
- $S_{solar}(d_t) = 1 + \alpha_{seas} \cos(\frac{4\pi(d_t - 172)}{365})$ accounts for solar declination peaking at the summer solstice (Day 172).
- $\beta_t \sim \text{Beta}(2, 5)$ is a stochastic variable representing cloud cover opacity, skewed towards clear skies.
- $\kappa_{cloud}$ is a regional cloud attenuation coefficient (e.g., 0.3 for ERCOT, 0.6 for ISO-NE).

### 2.2 Wind Energy Model
Wind generation $G_{wind}(t)$ is modeled by superimposing seasonal and diurnal oscillations onto a stochastic Weibull process.

$$ G_{wind}(t) = \text{Clip}\left( C_{wind} \cdot S_{wind}(d_t) \cdot D_{wind}(h_t) \cdot W_t, \ 0, \ C_{cap} \right) $$

Where:
- $S_{wind}(d_t) = 1 + \alpha_{seas} \cos(\frac{2\pi(d_t - 15)}{365})$ models seasonal wind patterns (peaking in Winter/Spring).
- $D_{wind}(h_t) = 1 + \alpha_{diurnal} \cos(\frac{2\pi(h_t - h_{peak})}{24})$ models diurnal breezes (e.g., night peaking in ERCOT, evening peaking in CAISO).
- $W_t \sim \text{Weibull}(k=2, \lambda)$ represents the stochastic wind speed distribution.

### 2.3 Hydroelectric Model
Hydro generation incorporates a distinct spring-runoff seasonality curve:

$$ G_{hydro}(t) = C_{hydro} \times \left(1 + 0.4 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi(d_t - 120)}{365}\right)\right) \times \left(1 + 0.1 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi(h_t - 12)}{24}\right)\right) $$

This models peak availability in late April (Day 120) and minor daily flow management.

## 3. Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) Dispatch
The simulator employs a heuristic "Greedy CFE" dispatch algorithm designed to maximize the hourly coincidence of renewable supply and demand.

### 3.1 State Space and Constraints
Let $SOC(t)$ be the State of Charge at hour $t$, and $E_{cap}$ be the battery energy capacity.
Constraints:
1.  $0 \le SOC(t) \le E_{cap}$
2.  $0 \le P_{charge}(t) \le P_{max}$
3.  $0 \le P_{discharge}(t) \le P_{max}$

### 3.2 Dispatch Logic
Let $\Delta(t) = G_{renewables}(t) - L(t)$ be the net renewable position.

**Case 1: Surplus ($\Delta(t) > 0$)**
The system charges the battery to store excess renewable energy.
$$ P_{charge}(t) = \min(\Delta(t), \ E_{cap} - SOC(t)) $$
$$ SOC(t+1) = SOC(t) + P_{charge}(t) \cdot \eta_{charge} $$

**Case 2: Deficit ($\Delta(t) < 0$)**
The system discharges the battery to cover the load deficit.
$$ P_{discharge}(t) = \min(|\Delta(t)|, \ SOC(t) \cdot \eta_{discharge}) $$
$$ SOC(t+1) = SOC(t) - \frac{P_{discharge}(t)}{\eta_{discharge}} $$

Where $\eta_{charge} = \eta_{discharge} = \sqrt{0.85} \approx 0.922$ (Round-trip efficiency $\eta_{RTE} = 0.85$).

## 4. Financial Valuation: Scarcity Pricing
To accurately value the grid services provided by the portfolio, the simulator implements a "Scarcity Pricing" mechanism for Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs). This departs from flat pricing by applying time-differentiated multipliers $M(t)$ to the Base REC Price $P_{base}$, reflecting the marginal value of energy during grid stress events.

$$ P_{REC}(t) = P_{base} \times M(t) $$

**Scarcity Categories:**
1.  **Critical Scarcity (Cat 6)**: Winter Evenings (Dec-Feb, 18:00-20:00). $M(t) = 2.0$.
2.  **Winter Morning Scarcity (Cat 5)**: Dec-Feb, 06:00-09:00. $M(t) = 1.4$.
3.  **Evening Peak (Cat 4)**: All year, 17:00-21:00 (excluding Cat 6). $M(t) = 1.2$.
4.  **Shoulder Daylight (Cat 3)**: 07:00-10:00 & 15:00-18:00. $M(t) = 1.0$.
5.  **Typical Mid-Day (Cat 2)**: Nov-Feb, 10:00-15:00. $M(t) = 0.75$.
6.  **Super-Abundant Mid-Day (Cat 1)**: Mar-Oct, 10:00-15:00. $M(t) = 0.45$ (reflecting solar curtailment risk).

## 5. Performance Metrics and Carbon Accounting

### 5.1 Carbon-Free Energy (CFE) Metrics
- **Hourly CFE Ratio**: The percentage of load met by carbon-free sources in a specific hour.
  $$ R_{CFE}(t) = \frac{\min(G_{effective}(t), L(t))}{L(t)} $$
- **Volumetric CFE Score**: The aggregate annual matching performance.
  $$ \text{CFE Score} = \frac{\sum_{t=0}^{8760} \min(G_{effective}(t), L(t))}{\sum_{t=0}^{8760} L(t)} $$

### 5.2 Grid Interaction Metrics
- **Grid Consumption (Brown Power)**: Energy drawn from the grid when renewables are insufficient.
  $$ E_{grid} = \sum_{t=0}^{8760} \max(0, L(t) - G_{effective}(t)) $$
- **Overgeneration (Curtailment Risk)**: Excess renewable energy not consumed by load or battery.
  $$ E_{over} = \sum_{t=0}^{8760} \max(0, G_{effective}(t) - L(t)) $$

### 5.3 Emissions Accounting
The system supports dual-methodology carbon accounting:
1.  **Location-Based (Average)**: Utilizes static annual eGRID factors ($EF_{avg}$) for the selected ISO.
    $$ \text{Emissions}_{loc} = \sum_{t=0}^{8760} (L(t) - G_{effective}(t)) \times EF_{avg} $$
2.  **Marginal (Hourly)**: Utilizes dynamic hourly emissions factors ($EF_{marg}(t)$) to capture the time-varying carbon intensity of the grid.
    $$ \text{Emissions}_{marg} = \sum_{t=0}^{8760} (L(t) - G_{effective}(t)) \times EF_{marg}(t) $$
    $$ \text{Avoided Emissions} = \sum_{t=0}^{8760} G_{effective}(t) \times EF_{marg}(t) $$

## 6. Conclusion
The 8760 Clean Energy Simulator provides a rigorous, stochastically grounded framework for evaluating the complex interplay between variable renewable generation, dynamic building loads, and energy storage. By integrating physical dispatch logic with scarcity-based financial valuation, it offers a high-fidelity approximation of real-world CFE procurement dynamics.
